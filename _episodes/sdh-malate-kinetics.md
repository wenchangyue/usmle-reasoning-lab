## The teaching experiment

Succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) catalyzes oxidation of succinate to fumarate. Initial reaction velocity
was measured across rising succinate concentrations, with and without a fixed amount of malate.

| Succinate (mM) | Without malate (µmol/L/sec) | With malate (µmol/L/sec) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 80 | 40 |
| 8 | 200 | 120 |
| 16 | 280 | 200 |
| 64 | 400 | 400 |
| 128 | 400 | 400 |

The best answer is **competitive inhibition in this experiment**. Both conditions reach the same
approximate maximum velocity of 400 µmol/L/sec. Half-max is reached near 8 mM without malate and near
16 mM with malate, so the modifier raises apparent Km while preserving apparent Vmax.

*This question, number set, and answer choices were independently constructed for teaching. They are
not from a real patient, commercial question bank, or recall source.*

## The one rule to carry out

Read an inhibition table in this order:

1. Start at the highest substrate concentration and compare the plateaus. That tells you whether
   apparent Vmax changed.
2. Find half of each plateau and identify the substrate concentration needed to reach it. That
   estimates Km or apparent Km.
3. Ask whether excess substrate restores the velocity.
4. Name the measured kinetic pattern before claiming a physical binding site or covalent mechanism.
