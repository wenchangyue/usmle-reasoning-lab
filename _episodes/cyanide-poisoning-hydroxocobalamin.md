## The original case

A 46-year-old man is rescued from a fire in a closed furniture warehouse. He is confused and
tachypneic, soot is visible around his mouth, his blood pressure is 82/48 mm Hg, and arterial blood
gas testing shows severe lactic acidosis despite high-flow oxygen. The emergency team administers
intravenous hydroxocobalamin and asks which molecular event most directly explains its benefit.

The best answer is **coordination of cyanide by a cobalt center to form cyanocobalamin**. The question
and all answer choices were independently written for this lesson. They are not copied or lightly
rewritten from a commercial question bank, a real USMLE item, or recall material.

## Build the mechanism before naming the answer

Cytochrome c oxidase, also called mitochondrial Complex IV, is a protein complex embedded in the
inner mitochondrial membrane. It receives electrons from reduced cytochrome c and transfers them to
oxygen at its terminal metal-containing center, reducing oxygen to water. The energy released by
electron transfer supports proton pumping. The resulting proton gradient powers ATP synthase.

Cyanide binds the ferric iron at the terminal reaction center of Complex IV. Oxygen can no longer be
reduced there, electron flow backs up, proton pumping falls, and oxidative phosphorylation fails.
This is **histotoxic hypoxia**: oxygen may arrive, but poisoned cells cannot use it normally.

As mitochondrial ATP production falls, cells rely more heavily on glycolysis. Lactate dehydrogenase
converts pyruvate to lactate while regenerating NAD+, allowing glycolysis to continue temporarily.
This explains the lactate rise; lactate is a downstream consequence, not a toxin made by cyanide.

## Why hydroxocobalamin is different

Hydroxocobalamin is a cobalt-containing molecule in the vitamin B12 family. Its trivalent cobalt
center coordinates cyanide to form cyanocobalamin. That direct binding reduces unbound cyanide
available to inhibit Complex IV. Recovery of electron transport, the proton gradient, and ATP
production occurs downstream.

|---|---|

| Methemoglobin Fe3+ is reduced to Fe2+ | Methylene blue in acquired methemoglobinemia |

## The transferable reasoning rule

When the stem names a drug, treat that drug as diagnostic evidence. When the question asks for the
**most direct** molecular event, stop at the first thing the drug molecule touches. Several choices
may describe real mechanisms, but the deciding question is: **whose mechanism is this?**
