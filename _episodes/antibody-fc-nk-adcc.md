quick_answer: >-
  In the standard teaching model, when IgG coats a whole tumor cell and killing depends on the Fc region, **natural killer
  (NK) cells** read the Fc through **CD16 (FcγRIIIa)** and kill by releasing **perforin and granzymes**:
  antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (**ADCC**). In the episode's question this is proven by the experiment:
  complement-free medium excludes complement, no lysis occurs without white cells, removing lymphocytes (but not
  monocytes or granulocytes) stops the killing, and a fragment without an Fc binds equally but kills nothing. Rule: ask
  whether the antibody uses its Fc, then what the Fc is coating.
faq:
  - q: Is the Fc region made of heavy chain, light chain, or both?
    a: >-
      Heavy chain only. In IgG the Fc is the paired CH2 and CH3 domains of the two heavy chains. Every part of the
      light chain (VL and CL) sits in the Fab arms. IgM and IgE have no hinge and an extra CH4 domain, so their Fc runs
      from CH2 through CH4.
  - q: Is every antibody's Fc the same?
    a: >-
      Within one class and subclass the Fc sequence is broadly similar; specificity comes from the variable regions.
      Between classes the Fc differs. In class switching a B cell keeps its V region and swaps the heavy-chain constant
      region, so the antibody grabs the same target but calls different effectors. Glycosylation, allotypes and
      engineering can also alter Fc function.
  - q: Why are PD-1 blocking antibodies IgG4?
    a: >-
      Pembrolizumab and nivolumab work by blocking PD-1 on T cells, which does not require Fc-mediated killing. IgG4 has
      little NK-cell ADCC and does not efficiently activate classical complement, so it lowers the risk of calling
      killers onto the T cells the drug is meant to release. IgG4 reduces, but does not eliminate, Fc-receptor
      interactions.
  - q: Do all antibody drugs work through their Fc?
    a: >-
      No. Neutralizing antibodies (bevacizumab; infliximab mainly), receptor or checkpoint blockers, Fab-only drugs
      (abciximab, ranibizumab, digoxin immune Fab), bispecific T-cell engagers (blinatumomab) and antibody-drug
      conjugates mainly act without Fc-recruited killers. Antibodies that bind a tumor surface to kill it, such as
      trastuzumab, rituximab, cetuximab and daratumumab, use the Fc for ADCC and complement. These categories overlap:
      infliximab can bind membrane TNF-α, and T-DM1 retains ADCC.
  - q: Which cell does the Fc call for different coated targets?
    a: >-
      As a memory aid: a coated whole tumor or virus-infected cell, NK cells (ADCC); bacteria and small particles,
      neutrophils and macrophages (phagocytosis); red cells or platelets, macrophages, especially in the spleen; worms
      coated with IgE, eosinophils (major basic protein); complement, C1q starting the classical pathway, which can
      opsonize or form the membrane attack complex. Macrophages can also engulf antibody-coated tumor cells (ADCP), so
      the stem's evidence decides.
  - q: If a question offers both NK cells and macrophages, how do you choose?
    a: >-
      Read the tool words and the experiment. Perforin and granzymes indicate NK-cell ADCC; engulfment indicates
      macrophage ADCP. Fc dependence alone does not separate them.

**Narrator:** An antibody, also called an immunoglobulin, or Ig, is a protein. It is made by B lymphocytes, and especially by their antibody-secreting descendants, plasma cells. **Narrator:** Picture a capital letter Y. The two heavy chains run the whole length of the Y, from the tip of each arm down through the stem. Each light chain lies alongside the upper part of one heavy chain, inside one arm only.

**Narrator:** Every chain has two kinds of region. At the very tip of each arm is the variable region, or V region. The heavy chain's variable domain, called V-H, pairs with the light chain's variable domain, called V-L, and together they form the antigen-binding site. The V-region sequence differs from one antibody to the next, and that is what decides which target an antibody grabs. We call that its specificity.

**Narrator:** Everything below the tips is the constant region. The light chain has just one constant domain, called C-L. An IgG heavy chain is longer. Walking down it from the tip of the arm, you meet V-H, then the first constant domain, C-H-one, then a flexible hinge, then C-H-two, and finally C-H-three at the bottom of the stem. C stands for constant, and H stands for heavy.

**Narrator:** Now cut the Y at the hinge. Each arm that falls away is called a Fab, which stands for fragment, antigen-binding. **Narrator:** Here is a point that students often get wrong. The light chain is not part of the Fc at all. Every piece of the light chain, V-L and C-L, lives up in the arms. The Fc is built entirely from heavy chain. So whatever the Fc does, it does with heavy-chain constant domains alone.

**Narrator:** Two classes change this layout. IgM and IgE have no hinge. Instead, their heavy chains carry one extra constant domain, called C-H-four. So in IgM and IgE, the Fc runs from C-H-two all the way through C-H-four.

**Narrator:** The shape decides the division of labor. The binding sites sit at the tips of the arms. **Narrator:** And notice where that reasoning stops. The stem has no killing activity of its own. It only matters if something reads it, a receptor on a white cell, or a complement protein in plasma. Fab grabs, Fc calls.
