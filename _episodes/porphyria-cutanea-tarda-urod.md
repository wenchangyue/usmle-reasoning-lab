## The original question

A 48-year-old man who runs a charter-fishing boat reports several months of painless blisters and
unusually fragile skin on the backs of his hands. The lesions often appear after weekends on the
water, rupture after minor trauma, and heal with small white papules and areas of darker pigmentation.
His family has also noticed increased hair growth over his temples. He drinks several alcoholic
beverages most evenings. He has never had episodes of severe abdominal pain, confusion, or limb
weakness.

Reduced activity of which enzyme most directly explains this patient's disorder?

A. 5-Aminolevulinate dehydratase

B. Hydroxymethylbilane synthase

C. Uroporphyrinogen III synthase

D. Uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase

E. Ferrochelatase

The best answer is **D, uroporphyrinogen decarboxylase (UROD)**. This case and all answer choices were
independently written for this lesson. They do not reproduce or lightly rewrite a commercial
question-bank item, real USMLE item, or recall material.

## Build the mechanism from organ to molecule

The large-scale chain begins in the liver. Markedly reduced hepatic UROD activity allows
uroporphyrinogen and partially decarboxylated intermediates to accumulate. Oxidation converts part of
that pool into conjugated, photoactive porphyrins, especially uroporphyrin and
heptacarboxylporphyrin. They circulate in plasma and reach the skin.

At the tissue level, these porphyrins absorb approximately 400–410 nm violet light near the Soret
band. Excited porphyrins transfer energy to oxygen, generating singlet oxygen and other reactive
oxygen species. Oxidative injury weakens superficial dermal structures and the dermal–epidermal
junction. Minor trauma can then separate epidermis from dermis and form a fragile subepidermal bulla.

At the molecular level, UROD is a soluble cytosolic enzyme. Its substrate is uroporphyrinogen III,
and its product is coproporphyrinogen III. Four sequential decarboxylations remove four acetate side
chains as carbon dioxide and replace them with methyl groups. The reduced “-ogen” intermediates are
not the final skin photosensitizers; their oxidized porphyrin products are.

## The transferable exam rule

For a porphyria question, identify the molecule accumulating behind the blocked enzyme. Early,
noncyclic precursors such as ALA and PBG point toward acute neurovisceral disease. Conjugated cyclic
porphyrins absorb light and point toward cutaneous photosensitivity.
