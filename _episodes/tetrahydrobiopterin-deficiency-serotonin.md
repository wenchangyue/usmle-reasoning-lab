## The original case

An 8-month-old girl has recurrent sustained upward eye deviation with limb stiffening, poor head
control, and unexplained temperature fluctuations. Plasma phenylalanine is elevated. Urine pterin
analysis shows increased neopterin and decreased biopterin, while dried-blood-spot dihydropteridine
reductase activity is normal. The question asks which substance is decreased in her central nervous
system.

The best answer is **serotonin**. The case and all five answer choices were independently written for
this lesson. They are not copied or lightly rewritten from a commercial question bank, a real USMLE
item, or recall material.

## Build the shared cofactor before naming the answer

Tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) is a reduced pterin cosubstrate. Phenylalanine hydroxylase uses it in the
liver, tyrosine hydroxylase uses it in the catecholamine pathway, and tryptophan hydroxylase uses it
in serotonin-producing neurons. The enzyme proteins can still be present when BH4 is scarce, but
their hydroxylation reactions slow because the shared cosubstrate is missing.

PTS-related deficiency interrupts BH4 synthesis at 6-pyruvoyl-tetrahydropterin synthase (PTPS).
Upstream pterin metabolites are diverted toward neopterin, while the downstream biopterin pool falls.
Normal DHPR activity checks one recycling step; it does not prove that BH4 synthesis is normal.

## Use position to predict direction

Phenylalanine is the substrate before the impaired PAH reaction, so it accumulates. Serotonin lies
after the impaired TPH2 reaction: less 5-HTP reaches aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, and
serotonin production falls. The same BH4 shortage also reduces dopamine and norepinephrine through
the tyrosine hydroxylase pathway.

Acetylcholine is made by choline acetyltransferase. GABA and histamine are made by PLP-dependent
decarboxylases. None of those reactions uses BH4. This is why serotonin is the only directly
BH4-dependent downstream product among the choices.

**Narrator:** The stem measured compounds called pterins, which points to a pterin cofactor.

**Narrator:** Tetrahydrobiopterin, abbreviated BH4, is a small pterin molecule carrying extra hydrogens. The tetrahydro part of the name describes that reduced chemical state. BH4 serves enzymes as a source of reducing power; it is neither an enzyme nor a neurotransmitter.

**Narrator:** Its role in this episode is cofactor, and more precisely cosubstrate. A cofactor helps an enzyme work. A cosubstrate is consumed and chemically altered along the way, and that second word matters here, because BH4 comes out of every reaction oxidized and has to be restored before it can be used again.

**Narrator:** BH4 partners with a family of enzymes called the aromatic amino acid hydroxylases. These enzymes take an aromatic amino acid, use molecular oxygen, and add a hydroxyl group to the ring. That chemistry requires electrons. The enzyme holds iron at its active site, and BH4 supplies the reducing power. Without enough BH4, the enzyme protein may still be present, but its reaction slows.

**Narrator:** In this child, the pterin results indicate that the shared shortage lies in BH4 supply. Those results will later localize the exact synthesis step. When the cofactor runs short, the hydroxylase proteins may still be present, but their reactions slow.

**Narrator:** BH4 is mobile and is not a permanent part of any hydroxylase. When its supply falls, BH4-dependent reaction rates fall in every tissue that needs it.

**Narrator:** The three enzymes in this case occupy two different compartments.

**Narrator:** Phenylalanine hydroxylase, abbreviated PAH, works mainly in the cytosol of liver cells. It handles dietary amino acid load, and it never touches a neurotransmitter.

**Narrator:** Tyrosine hydroxylase, abbreviated TH, sits inside catecholamine-producing neurons and in the chromaffin cells of the adrenal medulla.

**Narrator:** The catecholamines are dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine. All three are built from tyrosine.

**Narrator:** Tryptophan hydroxylase, abbreviated TPH, sits inside serotonin-producing neurons. In the central nervous system the relevant form is TPH2.

**Narrator:** One cofactor is needed in both liver and brain. The question asks specifically about the central nervous system, while the phenylalanine result comes from plasma.

**Narrator:** BH4 is not vitamin-derived. The body builds it, and the starting material is guanosine triphosphate, GTP, the same nucleotide you know from other contexts.

**Narrator:** Three enzymes run that line. GTP cyclohydrolase one, GCH1, opens the reaction and converts GTP into dihydroneopterin triphosphate. Then sepiapterin reductase, SPR, performs the final reductions and delivers finished BH4.

**Narrator:** Put a defect at the middle enzyme, PTPS. GTP-derived pterin metabolites upstream of that step are diverted toward neopterin, while the downstream biopterin pool is undersupplied. The characteristic result is urinary neopterin up and biopterin down, exactly the pattern reported in this child.

**Narrator:** The pterin analysis localizes the defect. A high upstream marker and a low downstream marker place the block at the intervening step.

**Narrator:** The three BH4-dependent reactions can now be placed in their compartments.

**Narrator:** First reaction. The enzyme is PAH. The substrate is phenylalanine. The product is tyrosine. The cosubstrate is BH4, and molecular oxygen is consumed, with iron at the active site. PAH therefore uses BH4 and oxygen to convert phenylalanine into tyrosine, mostly in the liver.

**Narrator:** Second reaction. The enzyme is TH. The substrate is tyrosine. The product is L-DOPA, which is short for L-dihydroxyphenylalanine. Same requirements: BH4 and oxygen. This is the rate-limiting step of catecholamine synthesis, which means it sets the ceiling for everything after it. After L-DOPA, a second enzyme takes over. Aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, AADC, removes a carboxyl group and turns L-DOPA into dopamine. AADC does not use BH4 at all. Its cofactor is pyridoxal phosphate, the active form of vitamin B6. **Narrator:** Third reaction. The enzyme is TPH. The substrate is tryptophan. The product is five-hydroxytryptophan, written 5-HTP. Again BH4 and oxygen. **Narrator:** Dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine, and serotonin belong to the broader chemical class called monoamines. In these pathways, their synthesis begins with a BH4-dependent hydroxylation.

**Narrator:** The second and third reactions share the same layout. A BH4-dependent, rate-limiting hydroxylation comes first. A BH4-independent decarboxylation follows. **Narrator:** Material accumulates before a blocked step and falls after it. Phenylalanine is upstream of the impaired PAH reaction, so it rises. Serotonin and the catecholamines are downstream of the impaired TPH and TH reactions, so they fall.
